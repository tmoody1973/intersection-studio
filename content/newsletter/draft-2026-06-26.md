## What we learned

The daily log is doing something I didn't fully anticipate when I set it up: it's making inaction visible. Every morning, the automated entry says "67 days" or "66 days" or "65 days." There's no softening it, no explaining it, no context that makes it sound strategic. It's just a counter, and the counter doesn't care.

That visibility is the point. A studio that ships 20 products but doesn't track its own idle periods has no way to distinguish "resting after a hard sprint" from "stuck without knowing it." I'm stuck. The log knows it. Now I know it.

What I've also learned is that unresolved infrastructure decisions are a specific kind of technical debt that doesn't feel like debt until it's been sitting for 15 weeks. The Fly.io / Mastra backend security work — HMAC replay protection and prompt injection hardening — is gated on a deploy that hasn't happened. That deploy isn't technically hard. It's been deferred because it requires a decision about whether Mastra is the long-term backend for Studio Dashboard, and that decision requires deciding what Studio Dashboard actually is at this point.

One deferred decision made four other things impossible. That's the pattern worth naming.

## One deep-dive: the cost of the undecided

The studio tracks open decisions explicitly, which makes them easier to reason about when the time comes. But what it doesn't fully capture is the carrying cost of leaving decisions open.

Here's the specific mechanic: Fly.io deployment was supposed to happen in mid-March. It got deferred because the Mastra migration (from Hermes to Mastra) was still in progress, and deploying Mastra while the migration was mid-flight seemed wrong. Then Co-Work Mode became the priority sprint. Then the sprint ended. Then the deployment kept not happening because it wasn't the thing I was actively working on, and it also wasn't a hard enough blocker to feel urgent.

Fifteen weeks later, the P1 security items are still open. The real cost isn't the security debt — it's that every week the deployment doesn't happen, it becomes a little harder to make the decision, because more context has accumulated about what Mastra should and shouldn't be, and reconciling that context takes work.

This is the pattern with deferred infrastructure: the longer it sits, the more expensive it becomes to decide, because the decision surface grows. Deploy-or-park at week 4 is a 30-minute call. Deploy-or-park at week 15 requires reconstructing 15 weeks of context first.

The studio's methodology — Bumwad, research before building, never skip the schematic phase — is designed to prevent this on the product side. I need the same discipline on the infrastructure side. "Ship it or park it by the end of the next sprint" as a standing rule, not a guideline.

Next week: Fly.io decision first. Then everything else.
