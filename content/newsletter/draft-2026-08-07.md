## What we learned

The newsletter about being stuck got stuck. That's worth sitting with for a second.

There's a name for this in systems thinking: the pattern appears at multiple scales simultaneously. The studio is in a dormancy. The newsletter about the dormancy entered a dormancy. The social queue — last human-authored post was April 20, 109 days ago — is in a dormancy. It's the same inertia, operating at every level at once.

The daily log kept surfacing one line that I kept reading past: "Publish the newsletter draft — written and sitting idle." That's not an observation about the newsletter. It's a description of the larger pattern wearing newsletter-shaped clothing.

Here's what the pattern looks like from inside it: each item feels like it will get done when the moment is right, when there's time, when the other things settle. But the other things don't settle. The moment doesn't arrive. Seven days pass and the newsletter is still a draft. The MKE Dashboard is still localhost-only. Canvas Copilot has been a committed-but-idle prototype for 32 weeks.

The failure mode isn't that work doesn't happen. It's that completed work doesn't cross the finish line. The MKE Dashboard is built. The newsletter was written. The TLDraw prototype runs. None of them have been released to anyone.

That's a different problem than not building. It's a shipping problem, not a building problem.

## Deep dive: The shipping problem

Building and shipping feel like the same thing from a distance. They're not.

Building is generative — you start with nothing, make decisions, write code, watch something take shape. The feedback loop is internal. You can feel it moving.

Shipping is a different activity. It requires exposing something to the outside, which is uncomfortable in a way that building isn't. It means the work can be evaluated by people who didn't make it. It means the version frozen at the moment of release is the version that exists in the world, not the version you still have time to improve.

That discomfort is real. It's not a character flaw or a motivation problem. It's a natural response to exposure risk. The question is whether the discomfort is load-bearing — protecting something that actually matters — or just friction.

For the MKE Dashboard: who does it help to keep it on localhost? Nobody's using it there. The data exists, the visualizations work, the multilingual support is implemented. The only thing keeping it from the people it was built for is the shipping step.

For the newsletter: who does it help to keep the draft in a private repo? The honest accounting of what dormancy looks like, the three-failure-modes framework, the observation about context loss — that's useful to anyone building with AI agents, running a studio, or trying to understand why things stall. It's sitting where nobody can read it.

For Canvas Copilot: a committed prototype with no direction doesn't become useful with more time. It becomes harder to kill.

The studio has 20 shipped products. Each of them went through this exact transition — from built to shipped. The shipping problem isn't new. The recognition that it's the current bottleneck is the first useful thing to know.
