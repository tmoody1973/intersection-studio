## What we learned

There's a pattern in music that maps pretty cleanly to what's happening here. A producer finishes tracks — fully mixed, mastered, sequenced — and holds them. Sometimes because the moment isn't right, sometimes because releasing means being done with a chapter, sometimes because there's always one more thing. The tracks don't deteriorate. But the window they were made for does.

The MKE Dashboard was built for a Milwaukee that needed better civic data tools. That need still exists. But tools have a timeliness problem: a neighborhood dashboard built in 2026, launched in 2027, lands differently than one launched when the work was fresh. The HOLC redlining overlay, the 30-plus data points per neighborhood, the four-language support — none of that changes by sitting. The political and civic moment it was built for does.

Six drafts. Zero sent. The newsletters document a studio that is still thinking, still honest about what's stuck. What they don't do is reach the people who might find the work useful.

There's a version of this that's still recoverable. The tracks aren't lost. They just haven't played.

## Deep dive: The security debt nobody's counting

There's a fourth item that hasn't made the top three but probably should.

The Mastra agents have had two P1 security items open for 22-plus weeks: HMAC replay protection and prompt injection hardening. They're running at intersection-mastra.fly.dev, handling requests against a Neon pgvector brain with 3,747 pages of institutional knowledge. No authentication on the replay attack surface. Known prompt injection vectors unaddressed.

P1 means it's a priority-one item — not a nice-to-have, not a known limitation to document. It means the system is deployed in a configuration the team already identified as insecure, and left it that way for five months.

This is worth naming separately from the "what's next" list because it's a different category of problem. Undeployed software and unshipped newsletters don't have a risk surface. A running AI system with known injection vulnerabilities does. The longer it runs, the longer the window stays open.

The cultural angle here matters too. Intersection Studio builds at the intersection of culture and technology. Part of that responsibility is building systems that don't exploit the trust of the communities they're meant to serve. A civic dashboard with security gaps doesn't fit that ethos, even if nobody's actively exploiting it yet.

This one isn't optional.
