## What we learned

Building and shipping are two separate decisions. The studio has been treating them as one.

Twenty products in the portfolio. A live Studio Dashboard. An MKE Dashboard with 30+ data points per Milwaukee neighborhood, 7 map layers, HOLC redlining overlay, and 4-language support. Ten newsletter drafts. All of it built, none of it distributed.

The newsletter is the clearest example because the gap is the starkest. Writing a newsletter takes 30 minutes. Sending it takes two minutes. The gap between those two things — the gap where ten drafts have accumulated — is not a skills gap or a technical gap. It's a decision gap.

At a radio station, content doesn't exist until it airs. You can write a perfect script and record a flawless segment, but until it goes to broadcast, it didn't happen. The audience doesn't know it exists. You don't know if it works. The feedback loop never starts.

The studio has been producing content for 186 days without broadcasting any of it.

## Deep dive: ten newsletters nobody read

This is the tenth consecutive weekly newsletter draft written for Intersection Studio. The first was July 31. The last was last week. None of them have been sent.

That's not a complaint. It's a data point worth examining, because it tells you something about how the studio's production model is working — and where it's breaking.

The model that makes sense in theory: an AI agent handles the mechanical work (writing, logging, tracking), and a person handles the judgment calls (editing, sending, deciding what to say publicly). The AI produces the first draft; the human ships it. Division of labor.

The model in practice: an AI writes ten drafts. A human ships zero.

The failure isn't that the drafts are bad — some of them are genuinely good. The failure is that the system has a missing step. There's no hand-off mechanism. There's no forcing function that requires a person to make the "ship it" call. The drafts accumulate because nothing requires them not to.

This is the same structural problem that's kept the MKE Dashboard undeployed for 186 days. The dashboard was built with a "deploy when ready" endpoint that required a human decision to trigger. Nobody triggered it. It's still there.

What both systems share: they were built to produce, not to ship. Production without distribution is a half-finished loop.

The studio's actual model — what Tarik has written about in the CLAUDE.md, what the methodology section describes — is "architect, don't code. Delegate implementation." That's the right model for building. It's not a model for shipping. Shipping requires the person at the center to make a call: this goes out now.

Ten drafts suggests that call hasn't been made. It also suggests the automation is working exactly as designed — it just wasn't designed to ship.

That's fixable. But it requires treating "send the newsletter" as a task with an owner and a deadline, not as a by-product of having written it.

The content is there. The infrastructure is there. The only missing piece is someone deciding it goes out.
