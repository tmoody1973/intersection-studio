## What we learned

Two months of watching the same blockers not move has clarified something: **undecided is a decision**, and it has a cost.

The Fly.io / Mastra backend deploy has been the studio's #1 open item since mid-March — now 14+ weeks. HMAC replay protection and prompt injection hardening (P1 security work) are both gated on it. The Canvas Copilot ship/kill decision is 60 days open. `useCoAgent` research was explicitly queued as "next task" in the April 20 commit and hasn't moved since. The MKE Dashboard is sitting with Housing tab IA, Hmong translation QA, and first deployment all unresolved since early April.

These aren't blocked on code. They're blocked on choices.

The other thing the log makes visible: the studio's architecture is sound. Co-Work Mode works. The agent system works. The MKE Dashboard prototype is demo-ready. The infrastructure that exists is solid. The gap isn't capability — it's prioritization.

There's also a subtler pattern worth naming: the sprint-and-pause rhythm is real and probably healthy for a solo operator running a studio alongside a full-time job and a radio show. The 60-day mark just means the pause has stretched into something that needs a deliberate reset, not more waiting.

## What's next

One decision unblocks everything else, and it's binary: **deploy the Fly.io / Mastra backend or park it.**

If it deploys, the entire P1 security queue clears — HMAC replay protection, prompt injection hardening — and Studio Dashboard can graduate from a development tool to something that could be demonstrated externally. That opens the door to documentation, case studies, and the first real external audience.

If it gets parked, the mental debt clears. The backlog resets. The studio can move to `useCoAgent` research, MKE Dashboard deployment, or something new without the weight of a 14-week-old undecided infrastructure item.

Either answer is a good answer. The only bad answer is "still deciding."

The lowest-friction re-entry point, if infrastructure stays deferred, is `useCoAgent` research — scoped, contained, no deployment required. It's the natural next step after Co-Work Mode anyway: the shift from the studio watching agents work to agents and the studio actually sharing state.

## One deep-dive: what 14 weeks of an undecided infrastructure item actually costs

The Fly.io / Mastra situation is worth looking at directly because it's a pattern that shows up in creative-technical work constantly — and it's not obvious how much it costs until you log it for 14 weeks.

The Mastra backend powers the agent layer of Studio Dashboard. It's running in the repo. The agents work locally. The blocker isn't that it's broken — it's that deploying it to Fly.io triggers a security checklist: HMAC replay protection for webhook callbacks, prompt injection hardening on incoming agent instructions. Both are real requirements for anything multi-user or externally visible. Both are scoped and documented. Neither is particularly hard.

But deploying to Fly.io felt like committing to "this is real, this needs to be secure," and that raised the bar in a way that stalled the decision. Classic analysis paralysis at the infrastructure layer — the kind that doesn't feel like a decision at all, just "not quite ready yet."

What 14 weeks of "not quite ready" costs:
- The entire P1 security queue stays frozen
- Co-Work Mode can't be demonstrated outside of local environment
- Canvas Copilot can't be evaluated in production context
- The studio's narrative stays at "we built a thing" rather than "we run a thing"

The architecture-to-running-software gap is where a lot of vibe-coded projects die. They get to "it works locally" and stop there, because shipping to production is where you have to make real decisions about real security, real reliability, real costs. For a product studio whose whole thesis is *ship over perfect*, sitting at "works locally" for 14 weeks is the wrong place to be.

The fix isn't a big lift. It's a decision.
