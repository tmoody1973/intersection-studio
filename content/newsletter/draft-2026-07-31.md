## What we learned

The automated log is more useful than it looked when I set it up. Seven identical daily entries is not just noise — it's signal. It names exactly what's stuck and why, consistently, without softening. Reading a week's worth at once is clarifying in a way that reading one at a time isn't.

What it surfaced: three projects in three different kinds of stuck.

**MKE Dashboard** is stuck on an action. It's fully built, demo-ready, tested. What's missing is one Vercel deploy command and two decisions — the Housing tab information architecture and Hmong translation QA sign-off. No infrastructure blocker. No research needed. Just time.

**Canvas Copilot** is stuck on a decision. The TLDraw prototype works. It's been committed and idle for 25 weeks. At some point a prototype with no direction stops being an asset and starts being overhead — cognitive and otherwise. Ship it, open-source it, or kill it. Those are the three moves.

**Fly.io / Mastra backend** is stuck on a blocker. Two P1 security items — HMAC replay protection and prompt injection hardening — are waiting on a Fly.io deployment that hasn't happened. That deployment unlocks the entire Mastra agents track. Nothing downstream can move without it. This one's been open 29 weeks.

Three different failure modes. Action avoidance, decision avoidance, blocker avoidance. All of them look the same from the outside: nothing ships.

## Deep dive: What 102 days of dormancy actually looks like

I'm writing this during a dormancy. Not a break — the calendar didn't pause, the log didn't stop, the projects didn't go anywhere. Just no forward movement on any of it.

What I've learned from watching the log accumulate: dormancy has a compound cost that isn't obvious in real time.

There's context loss. The Mastra agents track was deep in a specific technical problem (HMAC replay protection, prompt injection hardening) when it stalled. That work was all in my head. After 29 weeks, that context is mostly gone. Picking it back up will cost 2-4 hours of re-reading, re-tracing, and re-orienting before a single line of code can be written. That's not wasted work — it's unavoidable — but it's a tax that compounds with every week of delay.

There's decision debt. Canvas Copilot has been in limbo for 25 weeks because I haven't forced a decision. "I'll figure it out later" felt like a non-choice at the time. It wasn't — it was a choice to defer, and deferring has a carrying cost. The prototype sits in the repo, shows up in status checks, appears in the daily log. Every instance of it appearing is attention spent on something that isn't moving.

There's momentum asymmetry. Starting is harder than continuing. A project that's shipping generates energy; a project that's stalled generates friction. The longer a stall goes, the more activation energy is required to restart. At 102 days, the threshold is real.

The value of the daily log — running every morning whether or not I show up — is that it doesn't let me forget. It holds the state clearly and refuses to soften it. "102 consecutive days" is a fact, not a judgment. "Highest-value unblocked actions: unchanged" is a status, not a criticism.

That kind of honest record is useful precisely because it doesn't have an agenda. It just reports.

The studio has 20 shipped products. That number is real. So is 102. The work now is figuring out which one gets to change first.
