---

"Ship over perfect" is good product advice.

It's a terrible security policy.

Intersection Studio runs Mastra AI agents at a live URL. In November 2025, a security review flagged two P1 vulnerabilities: HMAC replay protection missing, prompt injection surfaces unguarded. Both got written up. Both went to the backlog.

That was 50 weeks ago.

A P1 isn't "would be nice to fix." It's the designation for a vulnerability that could cause real harm to real users right now. At 60 days open, security practice says you've effectively accepted it as permanent. We're at week 50.

I've been writing an honest newsletter about building a product studio, and this week's draft had to name it directly: a studio talking publicly about responsible AI and civic technology running a live system with known P1s for nearly a year is a credibility gap. Not a PR problem — a practice-what-you-preach problem.

The fix isn't complicated. HMAC signing update. Input validation and output filtering. Neither requires a major refactor. Both require someone to make the time.

"Ship over perfect" should have an asterisk: *unless perfect means safe.

What's the longest you've let a known security issue sit open? At what point did "backlog" become "accepted"?
